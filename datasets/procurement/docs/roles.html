Roles

Agreed 20 October 2026

This document defines three separate sets of roles:

  1. Consumer uses: what the datasets will be used for.
  2. API access roles: who may call the API and what each caller can see.
  3. Business roles in the data: the fictional people inside the firm, and what each may do.

1. Consumer uses

Each use reads the same core data. Anything specific to one use goes in a use pack under datasets/<name>/packs/<use>/. A new use adds a pack and does not change the core data.

2. API access roles

Locally, the caller chooses a role with an X-Role header plus a dev key held in the Worker's local secrets file. No key means public.

The API is read-only for every role. You rebuild the data by running the Python generator, not through the API. The plan's admin role is dropped because it would have nothing to do.

3. Business roles in the data (procurement dataset)

The firm's delegated authority policy is below. It becomes the approval_policy table. The limit is the largest single invoice, including VAT, that a role may approve alone.

Supplier contactExternal person at a supplierNot applicableSubmits invoices and asks for bank-detail changes.

Segregation-of-duties rules, which the planted cases test:

  • The person who raises a PO may not approve invoices against it.
  • A bank-detail change must be verified by someone other than the person who requested it, by calling the supplier back on a number already on file.
  • Each payment run needs two releasers. One is the Finance Director and the other is the Head of Finance Operations.
  • Splitting one purchase into several invoices to stay under an approval limit is not allowed.
