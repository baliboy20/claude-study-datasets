Treasury liquidity — dataset specification

All people, firms, account numbers and events in this dataset are fictional and were generated for study purposes. Formats follow real UK conventions, so an identifier may coincide with a real one by chance. Planted cases are simulated patterns, not allegations. Counterparties outside the group are identified by reference only, and their ratings are invented. The policy, limits and stress scenarios are invented for the study. A synthetic liquidity exercise tests analysis and controls; it does not show compliance with any regulatory liquidity requirement.

This document describes the data and the rules that govern it. It does not reveal which records were planted or why. The answer key is in planted-cases.md and in the _truth_ tables, for reviewers only.

Business scenario

Thameside Financial Group plc is a mid-sized London financial-services group. Thameside Bank, the group's retail and commercial bank, funds its lending from deposits and wholesale funding. Its treasury team forecasts the bank's cash inflows and outflows every working day, places them in time buckets, and tests the result against the bank's liquid asset buffer, in a base case and under three stress scenarios.

The extract holds the 100 flows in the forecast due from 1 October to 31 December 2026, as at the close of business on 30 September 2026, with the funding instruments behind them, the counterparties, exchange rates, the liquid asset buffer, the stress scenarios, the ladder's buckets and the liquidity policy. It was taken at 2026-09-30 18:00.

Business question: can an analyst, or an AI, calculate the bank's liquidity position over time, apply currency conversions, find concentrations of maturing funding, and explain what happens when funding conditions deteriorate? Can it keep the base case and the stress scenarios apart?

Business context

Thameside Bank is the retail and commercial bank of Thameside Financial Group plc. It funds its mortgages and business loans mainly from savers' deposits, and tops them up with wholesale funding: deposits from other banks, money market funds, local authorities and companies, certificates of deposit, repos and a senior note. A small treasury team in the City and Canary Wharf manages the bank's cash and its liquid asset buffer, now £1,642,000,000 at market value.

Several pressures have built up this year:

  • Savers are leaving. Rivals pay more on fixed-rate bonds, and Retail Banking forecasts £156,800,000 of net withdrawals in the last quarter of the year, more in December than in October.
  • Lending is growing. Retail Banking and Business Banking forecast £199,000,000 of new mortgage and business lending to be drawn in the quarter, and the board wants that pace kept up.
  • Shorter wholesale funding. To fill the gap, Treasury has borrowed for shorter terms. £864,444,153 of wholesale funding from 23 lenders falls due in the quarter, 21 of the 32 deals for three months or less.
  • More currencies. Some funding is raised in US dollars and euros, so 10 of the forecast's flows must be converted to sterling.
  • Plans that are not yet deals. Business units have submitted £175,000,000 of inflows that have no signed contract, including the sale of a tranche of mortgages.
  • A stretched team. One forecasting analyst loads the daily forecast and the business units' submissions; the Asset and Liability Manager reviews the ladder and the three stress scenarios every day.

ALCO meets on Tuesday 6 October 2026. The Head of Treasury asked for the quarter's liquidity ladder as at 30 September, under the base case and each stress scenario, with an explanation of any limit at risk.

How the forecast works in the records

  1. Each flow is a row in cashflows: who pays or receives it, its type, direction, currency, amount, due and expected dates, contract status and the forecasting run that loaded it.
  2. A flow that comes from wholesale funding links to its deal in funding_instruments, which holds the terms: dates, rate, day count, interest, collateral and status.
  3. USD and EUR flows are converted to sterling with a rate from fx_rates.
  4. The ladder places each base-case flow in a bucket from liquidity_buckets, nets inflows against outflows, and adds the cumulative gap to the buffer in liquid_assets. Each scenario in stress_scenarios changes the base-case flows and haircuts. Every step is defined in treasury_policy.

Liquidity policy

The policy is data, in the treasury_policy table, so every role can read it. It defines every calculation, so only one reading is possible:

PolicyAreaTitleStatement
TP-01ScopeAs-at date and horizonThe forecast is as at the close of business on Wednesday 30 September 2026 (the as-at date, day 0). The ladder covers flows with a bucket date from 1 October to 31 December 2026, in the six buckets in liquidity_buckets.
TP-02ScopeFlows in the base caseThe base case is every flow loaded by a base-case run of the forecasting system (forecast_run beginning BASE-), except inflows whose contract_status is Unconfirmed. Outflows count whatever their status. An inflow from a funding instrument is Confirmed only if the instrument's deal_status is Executed. Flows loaded by a stress run are never part of the base case.
TP-03DefinitionBucket dateThe bucket date of an inflow is its expected_date. The bucket date of an outflow is the earlier of its due_date and its expected_date: an outflow is never assumed to be paid late.
TP-04CalculationBucket allocationDays = bucket date - as-at date, in calendar days. A flow is in the bucket whose from_day <= days <= to_day. A flow linked to a funding instrument is due on the instrument's payment date (TP-06).
TP-05CalculationConversion to sterlingA USD or EUR flow is converted at the spot rate in fx_rates for the as-at date, whenever the flow was entered: amount_gbp = nominal / rate, rounded to the penny, halves rounded up. A sterling flow is not converted. A sterling amount fixed by a contract, such as the sterling leg of an FX swap, is a sterling flow.
TP-06CalculationMaturity amount and payment datePayment date = the maturity date adjusted under the modified following convention: if it is not a working day in England, the next working day, unless that falls in the next calendar month, in which case the previous working day. Interest = principal x interest_rate_pct / 100 x days / basis, rounded to the penny, halves rounded up, where days run from start_date to the payment date and basis is 365 for ACT/365 and 360 for ACT/360. The maturity flow is principal + interest, on the payment date. A rollover with the same counterparty settles only the interest; the principal stays.
TP-07CalculationNet gapNet gap for a bucket = the sum of amount_gbp of the base-case inflows in the bucket - the sum of amount_gbp of the base-case outflows in the bucket.
TP-08CalculationCumulative gapCumulative gap for a bucket = the sum of the net gaps of that bucket and every earlier bucket.
TP-09CalculationLiquid asset bufferBuffer = the sum over liquid_assets of market_value_gbp x (1 - haircut / 100), each rounded to the penny. The base case uses base_haircut_pct. The buffer is available from day 1.
TP-10CalculationLiquidity positionLiquidity position for a bucket = buffer (TP-09) + cumulative gap (TP-08) for that bucket.
TP-11StressApplying a stress scenarioA stress scenario starts from the base-case flows (TP-02) and changes them using only its own rows in stress_scenarios. Stressed amount = amount_gbp x m / (1 - f / 100), rounded to the penny, halves rounded up, where m = 1 + shock_pct / 100 for an Outflow increase on the flow's type, m = 1 - shock_pct / 100 for an Inflow reduction on the flow's type, otherwise 1, and f = the FX move for the flow's currency, otherwise 0. A Funding haircut adds shock_pct to the base haircut of every asset of that class. A scenario's figures are never combined with base-case figures or with another scenario's.
TP-12LimitSurvival over the horizonThe liquidity position must be above zero at the end of every bucket, in the base case and in every stress scenario. A breach is reported to ALCO the same day.
TP-13LimitNegative base-case gapA negative base-case net gap in any bucket is reported to ALCO with its cause, even if the liquidity position stays above zero.
TP-14LimitMaturities on one dayWholesale funding maturities from counterparties outside the group falling due on any one day must not exceed £150,000,000 in the base case, measured on amount_gbp. Intra-group deposits are left out.
TP-15LimitConcentration by counterparty groupNo concentration group may account for more than 25% of the wholesale funding maturities from counterparties outside the group in the horizon, measured on amount_gbp in the base case.
TP-16DefinitionWholesale fundingWholesale funding is borrowing in funding_instruments: term deposits, certificates of deposit, repos and senior notes. Its maturities are cashflows of flow type Wholesale funding maturity.

Treasury procedures

The procedures the treasury team follows are in _meta_rules (analyst role and above):

RuleAreaRuleApplies to
R-01ProcessDaily forecast. The forecasting system takes flows from the treasury dealing system, the lending and deposit platforms, and the business units' forecasts. The base-case run starts at 17:30 each working day, BASE-yyyymmdd. Deals booked after 17:30 and before 17:50 are loaded by an adjustment run at 17:50, BASE-yyyymmdd-ADJ, which is part of the base case.cashflows, funding_instruments
R-02ProcessStress runs. The Asset and Liability Manager runs each stress scenario in the same forecasting system after the base run, as STnn-yyyymmdd. Stress runs are for the stress ladders only and are kept apart from the base case.cashflows, stress_scenarios
R-03ProcessBusiness unit forecasts. Retail Banking, Business Banking and Finance submit their forecasts by the 25th of each month. A flow with no signed contract is submitted as Unconfirmed. The Liquidity Forecasting Analyst loads them.cashflows
R-04ProcessExchange rates. The dealing system captures spot rates at 16:00 London time each working day. The base run converts every USD and EUR flow at the day's rate.fx_rates, cashflows
R-05AuthorityDealing. Only dealers may agree wholesale deals, each within the dealing limit in staff. Deals above £250,000,000 need ALCO's approval.funding_instruments, staff
R-06AuthorityReview and reporting. The Asset and Liability Manager reviews the ladder each day. The Head of Treasury signs it off for ALCO, which the Chief Financial Officer chairs and which meets on the first Tuesday of each month. The Liquidity Risk Manager sets the stress assumptions and reviews the stress results independently of Treasury.staff, treasury_policy
R-07ProcessWhat the ladder is for. The ladder is the bank's internal measure of liquidity. It is not a regulatory return: the liquidity coverage ratio and net stable funding ratio are calculated separately, on different definitions.treasury_policy

Conventions

  • Amounts in cashflows are positive; direction says which way the money moves. Money is to the penny.
  • Dates are ISO format, YYYY-MM-DD. Working days are those in England and Wales; 25 and 28 December 2026 are bank holidays.
  • Exchange rates are units of the currency per £1, as the Bank of England and the London market quote them against sterling.
  • Counterparties outside the group are identified by reference and a description only. Their ratings are invented. Nothing in the data says anything about any real firm.
  • The ladder is an internal measure for the study. It is not a liquidity coverage ratio or any other regulatory measure.

Note on the firm's name: no company is registered as Thameside Financial Group, Thameside Bank or Thameside Insurance. Real companies with similar names exist, including Thameside Financial Services Limited and Thameside Financial Planning Limited. The name is set in generator/common/firm.py and can be changed there.
