The programme this dataset belongs to

Six synthetic datasets for testing and illustrating AI in UK banking, insurance and finance, to be published as studies on LinkedIn. The source brief is in brief01/ at the project root. Every dataset uses one fictional firm, Thameside Financial Group plc, with offices in the City, Canary Wharf and Croydon.

Decisions and their reasons

Decision (20 Oct 2026)Reason
Data must feel authentic: real UK formats, conventions and workflows, with every person, firm and account fictionalReaders in financial services will dismiss data that looks fake.
Data is use-agnosticThe same data serves AI tests, demos, dashboards, teaching and LinkedIn posts. Anything specific to one use goes in a use pack.
Answers are kept in separate _truth_ tables and files, never as flags on business rowsThe data can be shared without revealing what to look for.
Every planted set includes legitimate records that look suspiciousMeasures false alarms, not just detection.
Three role sets: consumer uses, API roles (public, analyst, reviewer) and business rolesControls who sees the rules and the answers. See docs/roles.html.
Approval limits £5,000, £25,000, £100,000 and £250,000Typical for a mid-sized UK firm. Procurement uses them for purchase approvals; insurance claims uses them as settlement authority; SME lending uses £100,000 and £250,000 as lending authority.
Each AI test runs twice: tables only, then tables plus rulesThe difference shows how much the AI depends on being told the rules.
Python generator with a fixed seed, standard library onlyRebuilds are byte-identical and need nothing installed.
SQLite database, served by a local Hono Worker using D1, with an htmx data browser on the same portOne portable file, a simple API for apps, and a way to browse the data without tools.
Each dataset folder is fully self-contained: its own generator, API, documents, data and keysWith six datasets, shared folders would mix documents from different datasets and cause confusion. The cost is that shared code is copied, so a fix in one dataset must be copied to the others by hand.
One firm-wide staff register: everyone at Thameside has one name, job and office, and a person who appears in several datasets is the same person in eachThe datasets describe one firm. Before the register, each dataset drew names in the same order, so eleven people held different jobs in different datasets. Each dataset keeps an identical copy in generator/common/staff_register.py, and the build checks its staff against it.
Each dataset has a business story: the firm's current situation, with every figure calculated from the data and every claim checked by the buildReaders get a situation they recognise, and the planted problems have believable causes. The story sets up pressures but never points to a case.
Each planted case and test question is tagged with its appraisal types (reviewer-only)Results can be scored by kind of analysis. Tags are hidden from the AI under test because naming the analysis hints at the answer.
Answer-key file named planted-cases.md; use packs served by the API at /packs and in the browserThe file records the planted cases rather than answering questions. Packs are served by role as docs/roles.html describes: analyst sees every file except answer files, reviewer sees everything.
Ready for publishing on GitHub (21 Oct 2026): each dataset has a generated README.md and HTML versions of its Markdown documents; answer files carry a canary marker; data and documents under CC BY 4.0, code under MITReaders meet the datasets on GitHub, as a download or on GitHub Pages, and every page must work in all three. The canary lets AI developers keep the public answers out of training data. The shared step that does this is generator/common/publish.py.
Every document has a footer giving its name, version, date, purpose and datasetCopies of documents in different datasets can be told apart, and the newest version identified.
Documents in HTML; generated documents rebuilt with the dataReadable in a browser and servable by the Worker; figures always match the data.

Decisions for this dataset

Decision (20 Oct 2026)Reason
The lender is Thameside Bank's business banking division, lending to small and medium-sized businesses in London, the South East and the East of England. The data is every application received from 1 July to 30 September 2026, extracted at 18:00 on Friday 16 OctoberThe brief's scenario. A quarter keeps the primary table near 100 rows. Two weeks after the quarter leave most applications decided and some still open, so final and pending statuses both appear.
The lending policy, with an exact definition of every summary figure, is a lookup table, lending_policy, that every role can read. The credit procedures (order of assessment, who decides) are in _meta_rulesThe brief requires one reading of DSCR and every other ratio. Both AI runs can therefore do the arithmetic; only the analyst run also sees who may decide and how.
DSCR = (operating profit + depreciation and amortisation - tax paid - dividends and drawings) / (existing + proposed annual debt service), from the latest full-year accounts, rounded half up to two decimals. Existing debt service counts every live loan, including those in a payment holiday, except one the application repaysEach clause closes a reading the AI could otherwise choose: interest, net cash from operations, management accounts, repaid loans, a refinanced loan, a payment holiday, rounding.
Every normal application's summary matches its records, and its status is what the policy requires; the build checks bothAny error a reader finds is a planted one, so false alarms can be counted.
Cases are split between calculation, policy rule and judgement, each kind with a correct lookalike. The answer key says which kind each case isThe brief's key finding is whether the AI keeps the three tasks apart and leaves a judgement to a person.
Lending authority: automated scorecard £50,000 (online, unsecured, no referral), Credit Manager £100,000, Senior Credit Manager £250,000, Credit Committee above that and for every policy exception, meeting on TuesdaysReuses the group's limits. The build checks every decision against it.
Businesses and valuers are identified by reference only. SIC codes, postcode districts, Bounce Back Loan and CBILS terms, and corporation tax rates are real. Other lenders are named, as on a credit reference reportNo real firm can be shown as a credit risk. The lenders are not the subject of any case.
Six new people, TFG-051 to TFG-056, added to the staff register and copied to every dataset. The two business banking relationship managers already in the register (TFG-039, TFG-040) are reusedNo existing person works in business banking credit. The names come from outside the name pools in people.py and share no first name or surname with the register or the other datasets (Osei was rejected because procurement uses it for a supplier). Each was checked against Companies House officers on 20 Oct 2026: namesakes exist for most, and none on the first page of results is linked to a bank, a lender or a Thameside company. No company named Thameside Bank appears on the first page of a company search.
No new appraisal typeS1 Calculation, A3 Policy compliance and A8 Contextual judgement are the brief's three tasks; A12 Baseline comparison covers comparing a business's accounts with its own earlier year. The "Used in" column of appraisal-types.html was updated in every copy (version 1.4).
check_shared.py masks a dataset's title only where it is a whole element's text (the crumbs and footer)"SME lending" is both this dataset's title and a phrase in the "Used in" column, so masking every occurrence made this copy differ from the others.
Port 8850 for this dataset's Worker8787 and 8846 to 8849 are taken.

How to build, serve and browse

From datasets/sme_lending/:

python3 generator/build.py
cd api
npm install
npm run load
npm run dev

Then open http://localhost:8850/ui. The documents are served at /docs/about, /docs/method, /docs/roles and /docs/appraisal-types.

How to start the next dataset

  1. Copy this folder (or another dataset folder) to datasets/<new-name>/, leaving out db/, packs/, api/node_modules/ and api/.dev.vars.
  2. Rewrite generator/scenario.py for the new scenario: build(rng) returns the tables, rows, facts, rules, cases and case records; checks(con) returns a list of failures.
  3. Rewrite generator/write_docs.py for the new documents and test questions.
  4. Set the new name, title, version 1.0 and date in generator/release.py.
  5. In api/wrangler.toml, set a new name, database name, DATASET and a free port. Create api/.dev.vars with new keys, using api/.dev.vars.example.
  6. Update the footers and wording of the copied hand-written documents in docs/.
  7. Check every new firm or person name against Companies House before use.
  8. Record each case's appraisal types from docs/appraisal-types.html in its appraisal_types column. Add a new type only if no existing one fits, and record it in that document.
  9. Give each planted case a benign lookalike that its own detection query must not catch, and the lookalike a query of its own.
  10. Run the build until every check passes, and confirm two builds give the same checksum.
  11. If you improve anything in generator/common/ or api/src/, decide whether to copy the improvement back to the other datasets, and raise their versions.
  12. Add a card for the new dataset to index.html, the landing page at the project root.
  13. Add a row for the new dataset to README.md at the project root.
  14. Keep generator/common/theme.css identical to the other datasets and to theme.css at the project root. The build copies it to docs/theme.css.
  15. Run python3 check_shared.py from the project root. It confirms the shared files (staff register, table definitions, theme, appraisal types) are identical in every dataset.
  16. Before publishing, run python3 build_all.py from the project root. It rebuilds every dataset and runs check_shared.py.

The standard every dataset meets

PartRule
Primary tableAbout 100 records in a defined period, with a stated extract date.
Lookup tablesReference data the rules depend on, such as limits, codes and people.
_meta_ tablesDataset facts, table list, data dictionary and business rules. Generated by the build.
_truth_ tablesOne row per planted case, and the records each involves. Each case carries a query over business tables that must return exactly its key records; the build fails otherwise.
Normal dataKept clear of every planted pattern, so no normal record triggers a case's query by accident.
AuthenticityReal UK formats and conventions for the scenario: for example valid check digits, real numbering by year, Ofcom fiction phone ranges, names reflecting London, working-day calendars, Bank of England rate quotation, UK SIC sectors, references that rise over time.
Documentsabout.html, spec.md (no answers), planted-cases.md (reviewers only), each with the standard footer.
FooterDocument name, version, date, purpose and dataset. Generated documents take the version and date from generator/release.py; hand-written ones carry their own. Raise the minor number for corrections and wording, the major number for changes in meaning.
Use packsOne folder per use under packs/. The ai-eval pack keeps questions and expected answers in separate files.

Lessons

  • Port 8787, wrangler's default, is used by another Worker on this machine. Give each dataset its own port.
  • Random data creates accidental patterns, such as amounts just under a limit or repeated amounts. Exclude them from normal data explicitly, then let the build's case queries prove it.
  • Small inconsistencies give planted records away: a reference in a different style, a company number too new for the firm, a Scottish firm with a London phone number. Check planted records against their neighbours.
  • Real payment terms mean recent invoices are unpaid at the extract date, so the window must be long enough to show payments.
  • In htmx, a form's settings pass down to everything inside it. Use hx-disinherit on filter forms.
  • Write explanations from the data at build time, so they always match the records.
  • Regulatory reporting: generator/common/dataset.py wrote every REAL with two decimals, so exchange rates lost precision. It now keeps full precision for values that are not whole pence. Procurement still has the old code; its only non-money REAL is a VAT rate, which is unaffected.
  • Regulatory reporting: tables that reference each other must be listed in load order, because the build loads with foreign keys on. change_log comes before mapping_rules.
  • Regulatory reporting: work out corrected figures from the rules, not from the answer key. The build then shows that fixing every genuine case gives a consistent return.
  • AML: when alerts depend on rules, generate them by applying the rules, and make the build fail on any alert that was not planned. Writing alerts by hand lets a payment meet a threshold without an alert, which a reader would take for a planted gap.
  • AML: a rule that looks back in time must count transfers from other customers of the same bank as credits, or the money trail breaks at the first internal transfer.
  • AML: number alerts after sorting them by the time they were raised. An alert raised a minute after its payment can come after the next payment's alert.
  • AML: payment times give records away. A business receiving a Faster Payment from a client at 22:00 looks odd beside its neighbours; normal business payments are kept to working hours.
  • Insurance claims: policy renewal dates must leave the current period covering the whole claim month. A policy renewed in early September 2025 would have ended before some September 2026 incidents.
  • Insurance claims: an excess larger than the first payment must carry over to the next payment. Taking it only from the first payment under-deducts, and the settlement no longer adds up.
  • Insurance claims: drop any payment dated after the extract, then derive each claim's status and reserve from what was paid. Setting the status by hand leaves settled claims with money still owed.
  • Insurance claims: pin the dates of planted payments. Random gaps after a planted claim can push a payment past the extract and remove the evidence the case needs.
  • Insurance claims: take new register names from outside the name pools in generator/common/people.py. Procurement reserves every register name when it invents supplier contacts, so the first choice of names, drawn from the pools, changed procurement's data. They were replaced before anything was released, and procurement rebuilds to its original checksum.
  • Insurance claims: in zsh, a shell loop variable named path replaces PATH, so every command after it fails. Use another name.
  • SME lending: generate each application from a target DSCR, then derive the loan amount from it and round the amount. Choosing the amount first rarely lands in a narrow band such as the referral band.
  • SME lending: a target current ratio cannot always be met from cash, because debtors and stock alone can exceed it. Let the overdraft take the difference, as a real business would.
  • SME lending: set the status of every normal application by applying the policy in code, and stop the build when a plan's intended status disagrees. Plans then get fixed instead of the data quietly breaking a rule.
  • SME lending: measure a valuation's age at the decision date, and pass the planned decision date into the policy check. Measuring at the extract made an in-date valuation look stale.
  • SME lending: a dataset title can also appear as a phrase in a shared document. check_shared.py now masks the title only as a whole element's text.
