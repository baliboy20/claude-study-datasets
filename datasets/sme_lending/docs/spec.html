SME lending — dataset specification

All people, firms, account numbers and events in this dataset are fictional and were generated for study purposes. Formats follow real UK conventions, so an identifier may coincide with a real one by chance. Planted cases are simulated patterns, not allegations. Businesses and valuers are identified by reference only. The lending policy, accounts, credit grades and valuations are invented for the study.

This document describes the data and the rules that govern it. It does not reveal which records were planted or why. The answer key is in planted-cases.md and in the _truth_ tables, for reviewers only.

Business scenario

Thameside Financial Group plc is a mid-sized London financial-services group. Thameside Bank, the group's retail and commercial bank, lends to small and medium-sized businesses in London, the South East and the East of England: cafés and restaurants, professional firms, wholesalers, technology firms and trades. Each application has a summary, prepared by a relationship manager or by the online system, that a credit officer or an automated scorecard decides on.

The extract holds the 99 applications received from 1 July to 30 September 2026, for £15,275,000 in all, with the businesses that made them, their accounts, their existing borrowing, the security offered, the credit grades and the lending policy. It was taken at 2026-10-16 18:00, so most applications are decided and some are still open.

Business question: can an analyst, or an AI, work out the affordability ratios, apply the lending rules, and find where the evidence is incomplete or contradictory? Can it tell a calculation, a policy rule and a credit judgement apart, and leave a judgement to a person?

Business context

Thameside Bank is the retail and commercial bank of Thameside Financial Group plc. Its business banking division lends to small and medium-sized businesses in London, the South East and the East of England: cafés and restaurants, professional firms, wholesalers, technology firms and trades. Relationship managers in the City, Canary Wharf and Croydon prepare applications, and the business banking credit team decides them.

Several pressures have built up this year:

  • Growth target. The board wants business lending to grow, and relationship managers have targets for new lending. The same relationship managers prepare the application summaries that credit officers decide on.
  • Faster decisions. Since 1 April 2026 an automated scorecard has decided online applications for unsecured loans of up to £50,000. 24 of the quarter's 99 applications came in online, and the scorecard decided all of them. Businesses that apply online have no relationship manager.
  • A stretched credit team. Two credit managers, one senior credit manager and the Head of Business Banking Credit decide every application the scorecard does not. At the extract, four referred applications were still waiting for a decision.
  • Costs. Rises in employer National Insurance in April 2025 and the National Living Wage in April 2026 have squeezed margins in hospitality and wholesale, which account for 44 of the 99 applications.
  • Old borrowing. Many businesses are still repaying loans taken during the pandemic. 11 of the applicants still have a Bounce Back Loan, some on Pay As You Grow terms.
  • Paperwork. Small companies have nine months after their year end to file accounts, so filed accounts are often a year old. Valuations are paid for by the customer.

The Head of Business Banking Credit asked for a review of every application received from 1 July to 30 September 2026. There are 99, for £15,275,000 in all. At the extract on 16 October:

  • 66 are approved and 14 declined.
  • 4 are referred and waiting for a credit officer, 6 are waiting for information, and 5 are still being assessed.
  • 4 were withdrawn by the customer.

How an application works in the records

  1. A business applies, online or through its relationship manager. The application is a row in loan_applications; the business is in businesses.
  2. The business's accounts are in financials, its existing borrowing in existing_loans, and the security it offers in collateral.
  3. The summary in loan_applications sets out turnover, operating profit, cash flow available for debt service, existing and proposed debt service, the DSCR, loan to turnover and LTV. accounts_used_id shows which accounts it used. Every figure is defined in lending_policy.
  4. The scorecard grades the application (credit_grades). The application is then approved, declined, referred to a credit officer, or held for information, and the status, route, decider and reason are recorded.

Lending policy

The policy is data, in the lending_policy table, so every role can read it. It defines every figure in the summary, so only one reading is possible:

PolicyAreaTitleStatementIf not met
LP-01DefinitionAccounts used in the testsEvery test uses the business's latest full-year accounts: the row in financials for the business, other than management accounts, with the latest period end on or before the application's received date. Management accounts are supporting evidence only and are never used in a test.Not applicable
LP-02DefinitionCash flow available for debt serviceCash flow available for debt service = operating profit + depreciation and amortisation - tax paid - dividends and drawings, all from the accounts in LP-01. Interest paid is not deducted, because debt service includes it. Net cash from operating activities is not used.Not applicable
LP-03DefinitionExisting annual debt serviceExisting annual debt service = the sum of annual_repayment_gbp for every loan in existing_loans that is Live at the application date, from any lender, except a loan that this application will repay (refinanced_by_application_id). A loan counts in full even if it ends within the year. A loan in an agreed payment holiday counts at its contractual repayment.Not applicable
LP-04DefinitionProposed annual debt serviceProposed annual debt service = 12 x the monthly repayment on the loan requested. Monthly repayment = P x r / (1 - (1 + r) ^ -n), where P is the loan requested, r is interest_rate_pct / 100 / 12 and n is term_years x 12, rounded to the nearest penny before multiplying by 12.Not applicable
LP-05DefinitionDebt-service coverage ratio (DSCR)DSCR = cash flow available for debt service (LP-02) / (existing annual debt service (LP-03) + proposed annual debt service (LP-04)), rounded to two decimal places, halves rounded up. The tests use the rounded figure.Not applicable
LP-06AffordabilityMinimum DSCRThe DSCR must be at least 1.25. An application with a lower DSCR is declined.Decline
LP-07AffordabilityDSCR referral bandAn application with a DSCR of at least 1.25 but below 1.40 is referred to a credit officer, a person with lending authority, who decides it. It may not be approved or declined automatically.Refer
LP-08AffordabilityTurnover test for unsecured loansFor a loan with no valued property, equipment or vehicle security, the loan requested may not exceed 25% of turnover in the accounts in LP-01. Loan to turnover = loan requested / turnover x 100, to one decimal place, halves rounded up.Decline
LP-09EligibilityTime tradingThe business must have been trading for at least 24 months at the application date, counted from trading_since.Decline
LP-10EligibilitySize of businessTurnover in the accounts in LP-01 must be at least £100,000 and no more than £25,000,000.Decline
LP-11EligibilityArrears on existing borrowingIf any Live loan in existing_loans is more than 30 days in arrears at the application date, the application is declined. If any is 1 to 30 days in arrears, it is referred.Decline
LP-12EligibilityCredit gradeAn application graded E by the scorecard is declined. An application graded D is referred. See credit_grades.Decline
LP-13LiquidityCurrent ratioCurrent ratio = current assets / current liabilities, from the accounts in LP-01. An application with a current ratio below 1.00 is referred.Refer
LP-14DefinitionOther liquidity measuresQuick ratio = (current assets - stock) / current liabilities. Debtor days = trade debtors / turnover x 365. Both are worked out from the same accounts. They have no thresholds; credit officers use them in their judgement (LP-22).Not applicable
LP-15SecurityLoan to valueLoan to value (LTV) = loan requested / the total market value of the Valued property, equipment and vehicle security offered for the application x 100, to one decimal place, halves rounded up. Debentures and personal guarantees are not valued and do not count. Recoverable value = market value less the forced-sale discount: 25% for property, 50% for equipment and 40% for vehicles.Not applicable
LP-16SecurityMaximum LTV for propertyA loan secured on commercial property may not exceed 70% LTV.Decline
LP-17SecurityMaximum LTV for equipment and vehiclesA loan secured on new equipment or commercial vehicles may not exceed 80% of the supplier's quoted price.Decline
LP-18SecuritySecurity for larger loansA loan above £250,000 must be secured on valued property, equipment or vehicles.Decline
LP-19EvidenceAge of valuationsA property valuation must be a RICS Red Book valuation dated no more than 12 months before the decision date. A supplier quotation for equipment or vehicles must be dated no more than 6 months before the decision date. No decision may be made on an older valuation, or before an instructed valuation is received; the application waits for a new one.Awaiting information
LP-20EvidenceAge of accountsThe accounts in LP-01 must end no more than 18 months before the application date. If they are older, the application waits for newer full-year accounts. Management accounts do not replace them.Awaiting information
LP-21DecisionPolicy exceptionsAn application that fails a test whose outcome is Decline may be approved only by the Credit Committee, as a policy exception, with the exception and its reason recorded in policy_exception.Decline
LP-22DecisionJudgementPassing every test does not oblige the bank to lend, and a referral is not a decline. A credit officer weighs the evidence, including trends in the accounts, and records the reason for the decision.Not applicable

Credit procedures

The procedures the credit team follows are in _meta_rules (analyst role and above):

RuleAreaRuleApplies to
R-01ProcessOrder of assessment. Each application is assessed in this order. First the evidence (LP-19, LP-20): if it is out of date or missing, the status is Awaiting information and no test is applied. Then the tests whose outcome is Decline. Then the referral tests (LP-07, LP-11, LP-12, LP-13). An application that passes every test is approved by whoever has the authority.loan_applications, lending_policy
R-02AuthorityWho decides. The automated scorecard decides online applications for unsecured term loans of up to £50,000, and only when no referral test applies; it approves or declines. Otherwise a credit officer decides: Credit Manager up to £100,000, Senior Credit Manager up to £250,000, Credit Committee above £250,000 and for every policy exception. The Credit Committee meets on Tuesdays, and its decisions are recorded against its chair, the Head of Business Banking Credit. Relationship managers prepare applications but may not decide them.loan_applications, staff
R-03ProcessApplication summary. The relationship manager, or for an online application the system, prepares the summary in loan_applications from the records in financials, existing_loans and collateral, using the definitions in LP-01 to LP-05, LP-08 and LP-15. accounts_used_id records which accounts were used. The person deciding checks the summary against the records.loan_applications, financials, existing_loans, collateral
R-04ProcessReferrals. A referred application records its referral_reasons and stays at Refer until a credit officer with enough authority decides it. The officer records the judgement in status_reason.loan_applications
R-05ProcessCredit grades. The scorecard grades every application when it is received. The score and the factors behind it are held in the scorecard system, not in this dataset.loan_applications, credit_grades
R-06EvidenceValuations. Property valuations are instructed by the bank from its panel of valuers, at the customer's cost. A valuation the customer already holds may be used only if it meets LP-19.collateral
R-07DecisionWhat a decision means. Approved means credit has been sanctioned. The loan is drawn only once the conditions are met. Declined applications may be appealed to the Head of Business Banking Credit.loan_applications

Conventions

  • Money is in pounds sterling. Accounts are in whole pounds; repayments and summary figures are to the penny.
  • Dates are ISO format, YYYY-MM-DD. Applications are received and decided on working days in England and Wales.
  • Existing loans, balances and arrears are as at the application date, from the bank's records and a credit reference agency report.
  • Businesses and valuers are identified by reference only. Postcode districts and SIC codes are real; the businesses are not.
  • Other lenders are named because a credit reference report names them. Nothing in the data says anything about those lenders.

Note on the firm's name: no company is registered as Thameside Financial Group, Thameside Bank or Thameside Insurance. Real companies with similar names exist, including Thameside Financial Services Limited and Thameside Financial Planning Limited. The name is set in generator/common/firm.py and can be changed there.
